## Character Notes

- Trung must shift from fight-or-flight instinct to building instinct. He learns that leadership means making small decisions about who sleeps where, who eats first, who guards at night.
- Mai defines home by children's experience: can they sleep, be called by name, say they hurt without being taken from caregivers?
- Binh needs very small beats: hiding under trucks, keeping food in pockets, choosing wall corners, drawing fence gaps instead of speaking, placing two fingers on Trung's hand.
- Hoang is not wrong about resource limits. He builds the fence, assigns watches, but warns that opening the base too wide will kill everyone. His hardness is necessary but must not become the first answer.
- Ong Tu Nieu stands in the kitchen like a defendant. He does not ask for forgiveness with words; he asks by dividing portions correctly and accepting supervision.
