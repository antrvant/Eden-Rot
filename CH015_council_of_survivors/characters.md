## Character Notes

- Trung's growth is not "becoming democratic" in a comfortable modern sense. He gives others the right to stop him even when his son is being hunted. He has seen what happens when one person can say "for the greater good" and lock a child in a room.
- Mai supports quarantine, screening, and truth-telling. Her compassion is structured. Mercy without rules becomes chaos; rules without mercy become the factory.
- Hoang's strike-first vote is not betrayal. It is an honest vote from a man who sees the world as a series of closing windows. The danger is that once outvoted, he begins to wonder whether council itself is a liability.
- Binh is old enough to connect pieces and young enough to blame himself. Adults must not discuss him as a strategic asset in earshot. But hiding everything also fails, because children hear fear even when they miss words.
- Worker Lead prevents the base from becoming Trung's household plus dependents. His challenge is not disrespect; it is the birth of citizenship.
- Hanh (Fake Mother) should hurt the reader. Her lie is awful but understandable. EDEN did not need to find evil people; it found hungry, frightened people with someone to save. This is how corruption spreads in apocalypse.
- Ong Tu Nhieu understands hunger and its effect on morality. His non-voting advisor role is self-imposed: he believes someone not yet forgiven should not hold a vote alone.
