## Character Notes

- Trung's growth is not "becoming democratic" in a comfortable modern sense. He wants to say no for his son but learns that protecting Binh does not mean deciding everything for him. His line "Ba dang hoc cach khong nhin noi so cua ba roi goi do la con" is the emotional core of his arc.
- Mai is not anti-science; she is anti-science-going-through-a-child-like-an-open-door. She proposes the consent ritual: Binh can stop at any time. She guards language: "Mau cua Binh" is different from "Binh la mau."
- Binh asks the questions adults cannot dodge: If the virus sleeps in me, am I still normal? Am I dangerous? Am I its? His consent is treated as a process, not a single yes/no. When he says stop, everyone stops. When he later says yes, it is because he wants to help other children, not because he was pressured.
- Doctor is not a mad scientist. He is the person most afraid of doing this wrong. He accepts council oversight. He corrects his own language when he slips into "the boy's blood." He fears becoming a softer copy of the Factory Boss if he lacks rules.
- Hoang is written with reason but lacking softness. He sees dormancy result as strategic: if Binh's blood silences the virus, Binh can walk through Edenrot zones. His line "If his blood saves people, keeping secrets is the same as hoarding medicine" is not evil but frightening in context.
- Worker Lead represents survivors who do not want one family's secrets endangering the whole base. He proposes council oversight of protocol without seeing detailed results that could endanger Binh.
- Ong Tu Nhieu humanizes: sugar water after blood draw, stories of children fearing needles in the old days. His line: "Blood is something inside a person, not rice in the warehouse."
- Hanh (Fake Mother Prisoner) reveals that the rumor about the "chosen child" does not come only from EDEN. A separate group, the Cult of Evolution / Prophet Dao's followers, believes the immune child is a "seed of the new world."
