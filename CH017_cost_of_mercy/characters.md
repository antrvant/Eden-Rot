## Character Notes

- Trung must not feel saintly. He should feel sick after choosing rescue because he knows Binh's bowl is smaller. His growth is accepting that good decisions can hurt those he loves and still be good.
- Mai's mercy is not speech. It is counting bowls, calming children, telling the truth about less food, and refusing to let children donate out of guilt. She embodies the cost of compassion.
- Hoang is dangerous because his numbers are accurate. He is not inventing scarcity. When he says "goodness cannot fill a generator," he is right. His flaw is that he increasingly treats anything not measurable as childish.
- Binh needs to learn that sharing does not erase hunger, and helping someone can make tomorrow harder. This is a painful but important step away from magical thinking and guilt.
- Doctor must be torn. Cure research could save many, but he cannot hold alcohol for a slide while a person bleeds. His decision to use lab supply on wounds should be costly but humane.
- Worker Lead's group eats less while doing the hardest work. Their resentment is not selfish; it is a signal that compassion without logistics becomes exploitation of the dependable.
- Ba Sau keeps the convoy standing through sheer will. She is the sixth person to stand up each time someone wants to lie down and not get up again.
- So 4 is not a number to Mai. The child's refusal to use a name is a survival skill learned from convoy headcounts where names made children easier to find and take.
